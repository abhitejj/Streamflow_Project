01

Introduction

Peninsular India encompasses climatically turbulent river systems characterized by extreme monsoon flashiness, complex Deccan basalt hydrogeology, and vulnerability to severe agricultural droughts. Across vital agricultural belts—including the Marathwada and Vidarbha rainshadows of Maharashtra, the semi-arid Krishna-Tungabhadra plains of Karnataka, and the flood-to-drought deltaic corridors of Odisha—water resource managers face an operational dilemma. Conceptual hydrological models require dense physical parameterizations that are rarely accessible across Indian basins, while simple statistical persistence collapses as lead times extend past several days.

Sub-seasonal river discharge forecasting (1 ≤ H ≤ 8 weeks ahead) supports reservoir operation, irrigation scheduling and drought early warning. However, existing statistical benchmarks frequently suffer from temporal target leakage, lack standardization in low-flow drought definitions, or fail to isolate the independent marginal value of multi-source meteorological observations.

1. Streamflow Discharge (Q)

CWC daily observed discharge stitched seamlessly with CAMELS-IND (1980–2020) and continuous CWC telemetry records (2021–2025). Normalized by catchment drainage area into specific runoff (q = Q × 604.8 / Area in mm/wk).

2. Gridded Rainfall (P)

CAMELS-IND daily rainfall (1980–2020) harmonized with IMD Pune 0.25° × 0.25° gridded daily rainfall (2021–2025: NetCDF4), reconstructed as catchment area-weighted means. Calibration against CAMELS-IND historical series yields a portfolio mean scale ratio ᾱP = 1.0009 and median weekly rainfall correlation across stations of r = 0.9998 (daily r = 0.9994).

3. Thermal & Evaporative Demand

GLEAM v3.6a Priestley-Taylor baseline harmonized with ERA5-Land reanalysis (2021–2025). Catchment temperature exhibits a Mean Absolute Bias of 0.92°C (signed mean of -0.035°C, r = 0.947), and ERA5-Land PET is rescaled via linear regression slope αPET ≈ 0.734. Temperature and PET remain gauge-point extractions because shuffling the atmospheric-demand group increases 1-week MSE by only +3.1% (versus +114% when shuffling precipitation features; single-feature share ~2.6%).

Quality Control, Physical Sanity Ceiling & Anthropogenic Regime Shifts

Catchments satisfy a strict missingness threshold (<10% total missing records). Any weekly timestep with fewer than 4 valid daily observations is masked to NaN.

Clerical telemetry errors were audited and rectified by enforcing a physical maximum discharge ceiling of ≤ 50,000 m³/s. At Ashti, a corrupted entry of 1,829,351 m³/s (on 05-10-2025) was masked and patched via temporal linear interpolation (max limit of 2 days) prior to weekly aggregation, preventing non-monsoon distortion during LSTM sequence imputation.

Unmeasured low flows recorded as zero (4 stations): after about 2010 CWC ran Karad, Warunji and Yadgir as monsoon-season stations, and unmeasured weeks are logged as exactly 0.0000 m³/s. Recorded-zero weeks make up 69% (Karad), 70% (Warunji), 62% (Yadgir) and 14% (Huvinhedigi) of the 2016–2025 test block, against 2.1%, 4.0%, 19.9% and 0.0% in training, and about a fifth of them fall in June–October, when these rivers are not dry. They are missing measurements, not droughts. At these four stations (marked †) any week whose issue-time or target flow is a recorded zero is therefore excluded from scoring for every model. This removes 100–440 weeks per station and lowers the one-week pooled severe-drought base rate from 0.238 to 0.065, close to the nominal 0.05. Haralahalli's zeros occur only in the dry season and also appear in training, so they are kept. Models are retrained with zero-flow masking enforced on training (1980–2010) and validation (2011–2015) folds, and log-target variants (M1-log, M2-log) preserve low-flow sensitivity.

Data Lineage & Pipeline Audit (2011–2020 Overlap): To verify that dataset stitching did not overwrite valid discharge, we audited all 8,740 station-days where CWC reported zero flow during the 2011–2020 overlap. On 82.8% of those days (7,236 days), published CAMELS-IND already recorded identical 0.000 m³/s, and 17.2% (1,499 days) were unmeasured NaNs; CAMELS recorded positive flow on only 5 days (0.06%). This confirms that post-2010 dry-season zero inflation was inherited directly from published CAMELS-IND records, ruling out a local pipeline merge error, while the underlying institutional CWC operational cause remains a documented inference.

Methodology & Anti-Leakage Protocol

We formulate operational weekly forecasting as direct multi-step regressions issued at week t (Monday 00:00 UTC) predicting future average discharge yt+H at horizons H ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8} weeks ahead. All predictors strictly terminate at issue time t, ensuring zero future meteorological leakage.

Terminal H-Week Purge Embargo Protocol

When issuing forecasts for horizon H, training records within H weeks of split seams would leak future target observations into the training fold. We strictly enforce a terminal H-week purge:

  • Train Set: 1980-02-25 to 2010-12-31 (Drop last H weeks)
  • Val Set: 2011-01-01 to 2015-12-31 (Drop last H weeks)
  • Final Test Block: 2016-01-01 to 2025-12-31 (522 un-purged weeks)

Identical-Row Evaluation Mask (ℳeval)

All model tiers (M0, M1, M2) are scored on exactly the same weeks: those where the target and every forecast exist and, at the four stations 𝒵 = {Karad, Warunji, Yadgir, Huvinhedigi}, neither the issue-time nor the target flow is a recorded (unmeasured) zero:

ℳeval = { t | yt, Qt, ŷM0,t, ŷM1,t, ŷM2,t ≠ NaN ∧ ¬(station ∈ 𝒵 ∧ (yt = 0 ∨ Qt = 0)) }

Absolute non-negativity is enforced via physical clamping: ŷ = max(ŷ, 0.0).

Forcing lineage note: 2021–2025 precipitation is catchment area-weighted, while temperature and PET remain gauge-point extractions; shuffling the atmospheric-demand group increases 1-week MSE by only +3.1% (versus +114% when shuffling precipitation features; single-feature share ~2.6%).

How to read the scores

Flow scores measure discharge accuracy. Warning scores measure which low-flow weeks were detected. Check the baseline, severity, and number of scored weeks alongside each result.

Metric guide · definitions and score direction
NSE · Nash–Sutcliffe efficiency
Higher is better, with 1 ideal. Zero matches predicting the mean observed discharge in the evaluation period. Negative values are worse than that reference.
KGE · Kling–Gupta efficiency
Combines correlation, flow variability, and mean-flow bias. Higher is better and 1 is ideal. Zero is not a universal benchmark. Compare against persistence and seasonal averages.
Log-NSE
NSE evaluated on log(1 + discharge), which gives lower flows more influence. Higher is better. This scoring transformation is distinct from training M1-log or M2-log on a log target.
CSI · critical success index
Hits / (hits + misses + false alarms). Higher is better, with 1 ideal. A hit is a correctly warned drought week. Correct negatives do not enter this score.
POD · probability of detection
Recall, or hits / (hits + misses). Higher is better. It is the fraction of observed drought weeks caught by warnings.
FAR · false alarm ratio
False alarms / (hits + false alarms). Lower is better. It is the fraction of issued warnings that were false, with issued warnings as the denominator. This differs from the false positive rate.
PR-AUC · precision–recall summary
The PR chart reports average precision. Higher is better. Its no-skill reference is the observed drought-week base rate for the same pooled sample.
Q80, Q90, and Q95
Mild, moderate, and severe low flow at or below the training-period 20th, 10th, and 5th percentiles. These thresholds are frozen before test evaluation. Counts refer to weeks, rather than whole drought episodes.
Station median and pooled score
A station median gives each contributing station equal weight. A pooled warning score adds the counts across scored station-weeks first. They answer different questions. Undefined ratios have a zero denominator and are not zero scores.

06

Conclusions & Policy Recommendations

This 46-year benchmark provides rigorous, empirical answers to the four core research questions governing hydrological and drought forecasting in peninsular India:

Finding 1: Horizon-Dependent Value

Weather inputs improve short-lead NSE

1–2 wk

M2’s paired NSE gains over M1 are +0.045 at 1 week and +0.034 at 2 weeks. No significant KGE gain is detected at these leads. At longer leads, seasonal averages provide a stronger reference. The table retains the exploratory, uncorrected tests, with their interpretation limits stated below.

Finding 2: Drought Early Warning

Flow accuracy and drought warnings need different checks

CSI 0.000

Raw-target M2’s median severe-drought CSI is zero at every lead. M2-log improves the one-week median to 0.229, while persistence reaches 0.529. The log-target model also reduces one-week continuous NSE from 0.486 to 0.464. Better warning performance comes with a flow-accuracy trade-off.

Finding 3: Zero-Shot Transfer

Zero-Shot Transfer Beats the Baseline Only at 1–2 Weeks

1–2 wk

Across the three held-out gauges, the pooled HydroLSTM beats the local baseline at 1–2 weeks. Weekly climatology is better at 4–8 weeks. At Kantamal, a three-feature flow-only GBT stays within 0.03 NSE of the LSTM at every lead. Transfer results therefore depend on the chosen reference, and the local baseline requires gauge history.

Operational Recommendations for Central Water Commission (CWC)

1

Check warning skill separately from flow accuracy

Persistence’s station-median CSI at 1 and 2 weeks is 0.615 / 0.494 for mild Q80, 0.585 / 0.422 for moderate Q90, and 0.529 / 0.379 for severe Q95. These are severity-specific summaries over stations where CSI is defined. Inspect the event counts and undefined ratios before using a forecast as a warning.

2

Keep both persistence and seasonal averages as references

Official M0 uses persistence at 1–2 weeks and weekly climatology at 4–8 weeks. The additional four-week lagged-persistence diagnostic has pooled Q95 CSI 0.251. Compare the models on the same scored weeks, and keep unmeasured recorded zeros out of drought event counts.

End-to-End Command Line Pipeline

To reproduce the benchmark, run these stages in order. Stage 1 needs the raw CWC, IMD, and CAMELS-IND files in data/raw/ (note that rebuild_rainfall_catchment_mean.py patches 2021–2025 catchment-mean rainfall directly from raw IMD NetCDF and CAMELS-IND shapefiles without requiring raw ERA5-Land files on disk); stages 2–6 run from the committed weekly tensors and models.

Model Overwrite Warning

Stage 1 rebuilds processed tensors. Stages 2–3 retrain models and overwrite models/. Retraining, especially the HydroLSTM, can alter the results. To re-evaluate committed models, start at Stage 4 (python3 scripts/audit_metrics.py). Use the saved tables and replay above to inspect the published results.

python3 scripts/prepare_weekly_dataset.py
Stage 1: Calibration & Features
python3 scripts/train_forecast_models.py --model all
Stage 2: M0 & M1 Benchmarks (pass --target log for M1-log)
python3 scripts/train_m2_models.py
Stage 3: M2 Hydro-Met & LSTM (pass --target log for M2-log)
python3 scripts/audit_metrics.py
Stage 4: Score every model on the shared evaluation mask
python3 scripts/export_benchmark_data.py
Stage 5: Site data file (medians, paired deltas, Wilcoxon tests)
python3 scripts/generate_interactive_plots.py
Stage 6: Interactive charts from the scored predictions
