Section 01

Introduction & Scientific Motivation

Peninsular India encompasses climatically turbulent river systems characterized by extreme monsoon flashiness, complex Deccan basalt hydrogeology, and vulnerability to severe agricultural droughts. Across vital agricultural belts—including the Marathwada and Vidarbha rainshadows of Maharashtra, the semi-arid Krishna-Tungabhadra plains of Karnataka, and the flood-to-drought deltaic corridors of Odisha—water resource managers face an operational dilemma. Conceptual hydrological models require dense physical parameterizations that are rarely accessible across Indian basins, while simple statistical persistence collapses as lead times extend past several days.

Sub-seasonal river discharge forecasting (1 ≤ H ≤ 8 weeks ahead) is essential for reservoir storage scheduling, transboundary water treaties, and disaster early warnings. However, existing statistical benchmarks frequently suffer from temporal target leakage, lack standardization in low-flow drought definitions, or fail to isolate the independent marginal value of multi-source meteorological observations.

Track A

Multi-Horizon Streamflow Prediction

Evaluating continuous weekly discharge accuracy (Q in m³/s and specific runoff q in mm/week) across lead times of 1, 2, 4, and 8 weeks ahead.

  • • Persistence Decay: Identify the lead horizon where hydraulic discharge inertia collapses (NSE < 0).
  • • Hydro-Meteorological Transition: Quantify where IMD precipitation accumulations and evaporative deficits surpass discharge memory.
Track B

Hydrological Drought Early Warning

Evaluating categorical early warning reliability across three standardized, non-parametric historical drought severity percentiles:

  • • Mild Low-Flow Anomaly (Q80): Weekly discharge at or below the 20th percentile of baseline flows.
  • • Moderate Drought (Q90): Discharge below 10th percentile (SSI ≤ -1.28).
  • • Severe Drought (Q95): Rare low flows below 5th percentile (SSI ≤ -1.64).

1. Streamflow Discharge (Q)

CWC daily observed discharge stitched seamlessly with CAMELS-IND (1980–2020) and continuous CWC telemetry records (2021–2025). Normalized by catchment drainage area into specific runoff (q = Q × 604.8 / Area in mm/wk).

2. Gridded Rainfall (P)

CAMELS-IND daily rainfall (1980–2020) harmonized with IMD Pune 0.25° × 0.25° gridded daily rainfall (2021–2025: NetCDF4). Multiplicative calibration (αP) over 2015–2020 yields a portfolio weekly rainfall correlation of r = 0.808.

3. Thermal & Evaporative Demand

GLEAM v3.6a baseline harmonized with ERA5-Land reanalysis (2021–2025) via FAO-56 Penman-Monteith reference ET0. Mean temperature bias is calibrated to -0.035°C (r = 0.947), and PET scaled via αPET ≈ 0.734.

Approval Gate AG-TS-04: Quality Control & Physical Sanity Ceiling

Catchments must satisfy a strict missingness threshold (<10% total missing records). Any weekly timestep with fewer than 4 valid daily observations is set to NaN.

Furthermore, clerical telemetry errors were identified and rectified under Gate AG-TS-04 by enforcing a physical maximum discharge ceiling of ≤ 50,000 m³/s. This prevented spurious outliers—such as the single corrupted clerical entry of 235,532 m³/s recorded at Ashti—from distorting tree splitting criteria or inflating regional specific runoff regressions.

Section 03

Methodology & Anti-Leakage Protocol

We formulate operational weekly forecasting as direct multi-step regressions issued at week t (Monday 00:00 UTC) predicting future average discharge yt+H at horizons H ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8} weeks ahead. All predictors strictly terminate at issue time t, ensuring zero future meteorological leakage.

Tier M0: Baseline

Persistence & Climatology

Non-parametric operational benchmark standard in river forecasting agencies.

  • • H=1w, 2w: Lagged Persistence Qt+H = Qt
  • • H=4w, 8w: Historical Weekly Climatology Q̄week
Zero trainable parameters
Tier M1: Autoregressive

Flow-Only Memory

Hydraulic state estimation capturing recession rate, peak flashiness, and seasonality.

  • • Lags: Qt, Qt-1, Qt-2, Qt-3, Qt-4, Qt-7
  • • Within-week std, min, max
  • • Recession slope: Qt - Qt-1
  • • ISO week Fourier harmonics (&sin;, &cos;)
Trained via Ridge & HistGradientBoosting
Tier M2: Hydro-Meteorological

Multi-Source Weather Memory

Full coupling of antecedent moisture, atmospheric demand, and thermal stress.

  • • Tier M1 inputs +
  • • IMD Rainfall sums: P1w, P2w, P1m, P2m, P3m
  • • Antecedent Precip Index: API30d
  • • Weekly dry days count (<1mm)
  • • ERA5-Land Tavg, Tmax, PET1w, PET1m
  • • Water Deficit: (P - PET)1w, (P - PET)1m
M2 GBT & PyTorch HydroLSTM

Rigorous Anti-Leakage Protocol & Validation Partitioning

Gate AG-TS-02: Terminal H-Week Purge Embargo

When issuing forecasts for horizon H, training records within H weeks of split seams would leak future target observations into the training fold. We strictly enforce a terminal H-week purge:

• Train Set: 1980-02-25 to 2010-12-31 (Drop last H weeks)
• Val Set: 2011-01-01 to 2015-12-31 (Drop last H weeks)
• Final Test Block: 2016-01-01 to 2025-12-31 (522 un-purged weeks)

Identical-Row Evaluation Mask (ℳeval)

To ensure zero missingness or selection bias across model tiers (M0 vs M1 vs M2), all models are benchmarked on the exact identical row intersection where all baselines and features are simultaneously non-null:

ℳeval = { t | yt ≠ NaN ∧ Qt ≠ NaN ∧ ŷM0,t ≠ NaN ∧ ŷM1,t ≠ NaN ∧ ŷM2,t ≠ NaN }

Absolute non-negativity is enforced via physical clamping: ŷ = max(ŷ, 0.0).

Section 05

Error Analysis & Hydrological Interpretations

Why do hydro-meteorological models maintain skill out to 8 weeks while simple flow inertia fails? We decompose predictive errors through mathematical decomposition of the Kling-Gupta Efficiency and evaluate the physical phase shift in gradient-boosted feature importance.

The Persistence Horizon Decay Cliff (H=1w → H=2w)

At H = 1 week, persistence (Qt) functions effectively for timing (r ≈ 0.63), but drops precipitously from portfolio median NSE = +0.265 down to -0.339 at H = 2 weeks. In flashy monsoonal headwaters such as Warunji (1,890 km²), persistence crashes to NSE = -0.665.

Hydrological channel storage memory in peninsular Indian rivers has an empirical half-life of 5 to 9 days. Beyond this time scale, discharge predictions that do not condition on catchment drying deficits (P - PET) suffer catastrophic quadratic error accumulation.

The Drought Early Warning Climatology Blindspot

At long horizons (H ≥ 4 weeks), operational agencies routinely substitute historical weekly climatology (Q̄week). While climatology produces a modest continuous score (NSE = +0.090 at 4w), its categorical drought early warning skill is identically zero (CSI = 0.000) across all moderate and severe drought thresholds.

Because historical climatology lies near the central tendency of the multi-year distribution, it mathematically cannot predict flows below the 5th or 10th percentile. Relying on climatology results in a 100% false negative rate for developing drought emergencies.

Section 06

Empirical Conclusions & Policy Recommendations

This 46-year benchmark provides rigorous, empirical answers to the four core research questions governing hydrological and drought forecasting in peninsular India:

Finding 1: Horizon Skill Extension

Weather Forcings Prevent Long-Lead Collapse

Tier M2 hydro-meteorological models sustain positive predictive skill out to H = 8 weeks ahead (portfolio median NSE = +0.290, KGE = +0.308). Across 10 out of 12 catchments, M2 outperformed flow-only autoregression, with dramatic gains in Ashti (ΔNSE = +0.317), Kudige (ΔNSE = +0.293), and Anandpur (ΔNSE = +0.063).

Finding 2: False Alarm Suppression

Drying Deficits Prevent "Crying Wolf"

Incorporating antecedent rainfall and evaporative deficits (P - PET) reduced severe drought False Alarm Ratios by 27.4 percentage points at 1-week lead (from 0.674 down to 0.400) and by 17.8 percentage points at 2-week lead (from 0.660 down to 0.482).

Finding 3: Zero-Shot Transfer

Cross-Basin Generalization Via Specific Runoff

Normalizing by catchment drainage area into specific runoff (q in mm/week) enabled positive zero-shot spatial transfer to completely unmonitored catchments. Deep PyTorch HydroLSTM demonstrated superior transfer in sub-humid Odisha (NSE = +0.403 on Kantamal at 8w), outperforming baseline persistence by +0.822 ΔNSE.

Operational Recommendations for Central Water Commission (CWC)

1. Retire Calendar Climatology for Sub-Seasonal Drought Alerts

Climatology (Q̄) has zero categorical skill (CSI = 0.000) for detecting low-flow emergencies at lead times past 2 weeks. River boards should deploy M2 machine learning pipelines to maintain early warning capabilities.

2. Integrate Operational IMD Gridded Rainfall at Forecast Issue

Coupling antecedent 30-day API and 90-day rainfall sums into reservoir rule curves reduces false alarms by over 25 percentage points, safeguarding against unnecessary irrigation cutbacks.
